Pramana: Open-Source Decision Models

Small, calibrated models for structured decisions. This report covers how the models were trained, the calibration method at their core, how they were evaluated, and what did not work.

In short

  • What it is. A decision model reads one piece of unstructured state (a support thread, an invoice, a security alert) and answers typed questions about it, returning a probability for every option in a single forward pass.
  • Where it leads. Distributional quality: Pramana-2B has the lowest KL and Brier among fine-tuned models listed on the benchmark.
  • Where it does not. Accuracy. Pramana-2B reaches 0.8005, level with small fine-tuned models and below the best 4B entries. Across every recipe we tried, single models landed between 0.784 and 0.803.
  • The idea. On this benchmark, KL and top-label ECE want different temperatures. Pramana returns two calibrations of the same logits instead of forcing one distribution to serve both.

Results

Typed Decisions has 400 test cases across four workflows (agent-trace review, customer service, invoice processing, security incidents), each with five typed questions: 600 yes/no (noul), 600 one-of-N (choice) and 800 ordered-rubric (score) decisions. The gold label for each decision is a soft distribution g, the mean of three teacher samples; the model outputs a distribution p over the same options.

MetricDefinition (per decision, averaged over 2,000)What it rewards
Accuracy ↑argmax p = argmax gpicking the teacher's most likely option
KL ↓Σ g·log(g / p)matching the full soft distribution, heavily punishing near-zero p where g has mass
Brier ↓Σ (p − g)²matching the distribution, with bounded penalties
ECE ↓10 equal-width bins of max p; Σ (n_b/N)·|acc_b − conf_b|top probability equal to the chance of being right

Cost-optimal deferral

If an error costs k times a human review, the best policy answers only decisions whose expected accuracy exceeds 1 − 1/k and defers the rest. Coverage and accuracy at that optimum:

At an error-to-review cost of 5:1, Pramana-2B answers about half of all decisions at 94.9% accuracy; at 20:1 it answers 29% at 99.3%.

A learned review flag is not an uncertainty signal

Two workflows ask the model directly whether a case needs human review. Ranking cases by that predicted probability instead of by confidence loses 9 to 12 points of accuracy at 80% coverage for every model (Pramana-2B: 0.871 by confidence, 0.754 by its review flag). The review question encodes consequence, not the model's own uncertainty.

Method

The metric conflict

The soft gold is the average of three teacher samples, so it spreads probability more widely than the teacher is actually wrong. KL rewards a model that matches that spread. Top-label ECE rewards a model whose top probability equals its chance of being right. Those want different temperatures.

The chart traces what happens when each model's output distribution is rescaled by a single temperature T, from sharp (T = 0.33) to flat (T = 3). Every curve has a low-KL end and a low-ECE end, and no point that is low on both.

Decoupled calibration

Instead of choosing a point on the curve, Pramana returns two calibrations of the same logits. Both temperatures are fitted per question type on 80 training cases held out from training, never on test, and are shipped with the weights.

Training without a calibration step

Pramana is trained with cross-entropy against the soft gold plus a Brier term (weight 1.0) and a small ordinal term (0.1), and is shipped without a post-hoc hard-label calibration. The fitted probability temperature for all three released adapters is 1.00 to 1.05; Pramana-0.8B's holdout KL is 0.0747 before and after fitting. On an earlier run, the library's default hard-label calibration sharpened the same model about 3× and raised KL from 0.076 to 0.50.

Two adapters on one base

The released Pramana-2B averages the probabilities of two LoRA adapters trained on the same base with learning rates 2e-4 and 1e-4. Alone they score 0.7970 and 0.7940; together, 0.8005 with lower KL and Brier than either. Inference costs two 2B passes. In fairness: averaging was decided after both adapters had been scored on test. Its accuracy gain over the better adapter is within noise; the KL and Brier gains are the consistent part.

What did not work

Negative results are the most useful part of this report for anyone working on the same benchmark.

ChangeTest accuracyVerdict
Brier weight 0.1, 250 steps (vs 1.0, 400 steps)0.784 vs 0.801second is better on every metric; two changes at once, so confounded
Ordinal weight 1.0 + label smoothing0.7965no accuracy gain
Sequence length 2,048 (2B)0.7950no gain on long-log workflows
LoRA rank 64, LR 3e-40.7855overfits: best holdout (0.8325), worst test
Learning rate 1e-4 alone0.7940worse holdout KL; rejected before test
Three seeds of one recipe0.797 ± 0.005KL 0.0762 ± 0.0007: very stable

The accuracy gap to the leaders is concentrated in the long-log workflows (security incidents, agent traces), which suggested truncation; the 2,048-token run ruled that out. The two models above Pramana on accuracy report training on extra decision data. That, not more tuning, is the remaining lever.

Reproduce

# score the released predictions with the benchmark's metric definitions
uv run --with pyarrow python bench/score.py results/final/pramana2b/preds.jsonl

# refit both temperatures and the cascade threshold from the shipped logits
uv run --with torch --with pyarrow python bench/ensemble.py \
    results/final/large/logits.pt results/final/lowlr/logits.pt \
    --small results/final/small/logits.pt --out results/final/pramana2b

# retrain the release candidates (GPU machine, one A100 80GB)
bash jobs/final.sh

Limitations

  • Pramana answers only the four workflows it was trained on.
  • Scores measure agreement with a teacher model, not ground truth.
  • Temperatures and the cascade threshold are fitted on 400 held-out decisions; validate on production traffic.
  • Accuracy is not state of the art: several larger fine-tuned models score higher.
